<h2>A turn towards re</h2>
<div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">ر</div><p><b>Re</b> makes a Persian r sound, often a brief tongue tap. It has no dots. Like alef and dâl, it does not connect to the following letter.</p><div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">دَر</div><p class="gloss">dar · door</p><p>The small mark above dâl supplies short a. Both letters remain unjoined.</p><div class="task"><strong>Try 17.</strong> Copy door. Keep the short-vowel mark above dâl. <h2>A dot above: nun</h2>
<div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">ن / نـ / ـنـ / ـن</div><p><b>Nun</b> makes <b>n</b>. It has one dot above. It can connect on both sides when neighbours allow.</p><p>Beginning nun and beginning be are especially easy to confuse:</p><div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">نـ &nbsp; بـ</div><div class="task"><strong>Try 18.</strong> In the pair above, identify nun by its dot above. Copy beginning nun and beginning be, keeping their dots in the correct places.</div><div class="write-lines"><div></div><div></div></div><p class="note">Separate be and nun are not identical bowls. Compare the same positional forms when playing dot games.</p>
