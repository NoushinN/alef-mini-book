<h2>Meet dâl. Read wind.</h2>
<div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">د</div><p><b>Dâl</b> makes the sound <b>d</b>. It has no dots and does not connect to the following letter.</p><div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">باد</div><p class="gloss">bâd · wind</p><p>Here be joins to alef. The last letter is dâl. <h2>Three little vowel guides</h2>

<h2>Dots stay. Vowel hints may go.</h2>
<p>The dot under be identifies the letter. A short-vowel mark tells you which vowel to say after it.</p><div class="reading" lang="fa" dir="rtl">بَ &nbsp; بِ &nbsp; بُ</div><p>Every example above has the same letter dot. Only the short-vowel guide changes.</p><div class="task"><strong>Try 11.</strong> Write ba, be and bo using be plus the correct short-vowel mark. Keep the letter’s dot in every model.</div><div class="write-lines"><div></div><div></div><div></div></div><p class="note">These are practice syllables, not vocabulary to translate. Do not guess an unfamiliar word’s vowels from its unmarked spelling alone.</p>
